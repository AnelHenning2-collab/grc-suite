Mnemonics & Memory Tricks

These are the ones that actually appear on the exam. Memorize the mnemonic, recall the concept.

The Big Ones

CIA
Confidentiality · Integrity · Availability

The three pillars of information security. Every security decision traces back to one of these three.

AAA
Authentication · Authorization · Accounting

Who are you? What can you do? What did you do? — The access control framework.

PICEL
Prepare · Identify · Contain · Eradicate · Lessons Learned

Incident Response steps. Add Recover between Eradicate and Lessons: PICREL.

KHAL
Know · Have · Are · Location

The four authentication factors. Something you Know, Have, Are, or where you Are.

IDS vs IPS — The One Everyone Confuses

IDS = Camera. Watches. Records. Alerts. Does NOT stop anything.
IPS = Security Guard. Watches AND physically stops the threat.

Memory trick: IDS Detects. IPS Prevents. The D and P tell you everything.

Hot / Warm / Cold Sites

Think of it like a car:

Hot site = Car engine running, ready to drive immediately. Most expensive.
Warm site = Car in the garage, needs 30 min warmup. Moderate cost.
Cold site = Empty parking space. Cheapest — but days to get operational.

RTO vs RPO

RTO (Recovery Time Objective) = How long can we be DOWN?
Think: "How long until we're back up?"

RPO (Recovery Point Objective) = How much DATA can we lose?
Think: "How far back does our backup go?"

Anchor: RPO is about the PAST (last backup). RTO is about the FUTURE (how soon we recover).

MAC vs DAC vs RBAC

MAC (Mandatory) = Military. The SYSTEM decides based on labels. You can't override it.
DAC (Discretionary) = Desktop. The DATA OWNER decides who gets access. (Like sharing a Google Doc.)
RBAC (Role-Based) = Real World. Access based on your JOB ROLE. Manager vs cashier.

Memory: M = Mandatory/Military. D = Discretionary/Data Owner. R = Role.

Encryption Quick Reference

TypeKeysSpeedUse CaseExamples
